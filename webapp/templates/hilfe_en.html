How to use every page – and how each displayed value is calculated.

Quick start ↑ back to top

The page 🚀 Setup guides you step by step through the basic settings and shows for each step whether it is already done. In short:

  1. Settings → Vehicle picture: choose a picture from the gallery or upload your own – instructions and a prompt for image AIs under vehicle pictures.
  2. Settings → Calculation parameters: vehicle name, comparison with a petrol, diesel or LPG car, its consumption, PV price and vehicle tax. The language (German/English) is set here as well.
  3. Connection to Home Assistant: automatic in the add-on, otherwise URL and long-lived token.
  4. Sensors: odometer, PV and grid into car (kWh), {{ kf.name }} price, optionally battery level – easiest via the search.
  5. Database (optional): only if it goes back further than HA.
  6. Create an electricity tariff, then fetch the past months under HA Import.
  7. Charging or Invoices: add charging on the road; Tax & GHG and Reports as needed.
Save first, then test: the connection test and the database search use the saved credentials. So after entering them, first click “Save all settings” – otherwise the old values are tested.
After that it largely runs by itself: the nightly fetch automatically collects km, PV/grid kWh and {{ kf.name }} price for the current and the previous month.

Basic concept ↑ back to top

EV Tracker compares your actual electricity costs with the extrapolated costs of an imaginary {{ kf.fahrzeug_gen }} that would have driven the same kilometres. The difference is the saving.

SideData source
EV (real)Sum of all recorded charging sessions – from HA import, invoice import or manual entry.
{{ kf.fahrzeug[:1] | upper }}{{ kf.fahrzeug[1:] }} (notional)km driven per month × {{ kf.fahrzeug }} consumption × {{ kf.name }} price of the month, added up.

Plus two bonus items: the vehicle tax you no longer pay and the GHG quota (German “THG-Quote”, a yearly premium for EV owners). The avoided CO2 is shown as well.

Monthly grid: kilometres and {{ kf.name }} prices are kept per month – one value per month, no individual trips. Charging sessions, on the other hand, have a day date. Automatically imported charging is therefore booked on the first day of the month.

Choosing a period

The selection field below the header limits all key figures and charts to one period: entire period, a calendar year, a quarter of the current or last year, or a summer or winter half-year. The choice is remembered in the browser and still applies next time; “✕ Entire period” resets it. The sliders in the individual charts narrow it down further within the selected period.

PeriodMonths
YearJanuary to December, in the current year up to the current month.
QuarterQ1 Jan–Mar, Q2 Apr–Jun, Q3 Jul–Sep, Q4 Oct–Dec.
SummerApril to September.
WinterOctober to March of the following year – “Winter 2025/26” is Oct 2025 to Mar 2026.
Vehicle taxTax savings = vehicle tax [€/year] × months in period ÷ 12 − EV vehicle tax [€/year] × taxable months ÷ 12
Why pro rata? Vehicle tax is an annual amount. Otherwise every quarter would be credited with a whole year's tax – and several years only once. For the entire period, the months from the first to the last month with data count. From the start of the period, the electricity tariff history shows the tariff valid then, even if it was entered earlier; tariff changes within the period appear as a step.

Statistics ↑ back to top

Compares two periods side by side – e.g. a year with the previous year or summer against winter. The quick selection at the top offers the most common pairs; “⇄ swap” switches the sides.

Table: all key figures of both periods, plus the difference (first minus second period) absolute and in per cent. Green means better, orange worse: for consumption, costs, electricity price and grid and public share a minus is good, for savings, PV share, GHG income and avoided CO2 a plus. Values without better or worse stay without colour, e.g. km, kWh charged or the {{ kf.name }} price. Shares (PV, grid, public) are compared in percentage points.

Totals depend on the length: a year has more km and higher costs than a quarter. For fair comparisons of periods of different length use consumption, costs per 100 km and Ø electricity price.

Month by month: chart with a selectable key figure. The first month of one period is shown next to the first month of the other; for summer against winter, April next to October (“Apr · Oct”).

Consumption from the battery level per month: the same pairing as a table with the figures behind it – per month and period the counted km, the kWh from the battery and the consumption. The total row is weighted via Σ kWh ÷ Σ km and matches the row “Consumption from battery” in the table above.

Different km than in the chart: this table only counts the km of the counted trip segments, not the monthly recorded total kilometres – otherwise km, kWh and consumption in one row would not match. Which segments are left out is explained under Consumption from the battery level.

Trips ↑ back to top

Heading: “🚗 Kilometres driven (monthly)”

  • One km value per month. Saving the same month again overwrites the old value.
  • Normally comes automatically from HA (difference of the odometer) – adding it manually is possible at any time.
  • Comma and point are both allowed as decimal separator. For km and euro amounts a thousands point is recognised (“1.234” = 1234 km, “1.234,50” €).
{{ kf.name }} equivalentLitres = km ÷ 100 × {{ kf.fahrzeug }} consumption [L/100km]
CO2CO2 [kg] = litres × CO2 factor [kg/L]
Example – 791 km at 7,0 L/100 km and 2,37 kg/L: 791 ÷ 100 × 7,0 = 55,37 L 55,37 × 2,37 = 131,2 kg CO2

Consumption analysis (kWh/100 km)

Shows the lowest, highest and average monthly consumption. Only months with both km and charging sessions are taken into account.

Consumption per monthConsumption = kWh (month) ÷ km (month) × 100
Average – weightedØ = Σ kWh (all months) ÷ Σ km (all months) × 100
Weighted, not the mean of the monthly values: a month with 2.000 km counts more than one with 200 km. Otherwise a short winter month would distort the average heavily.

Consumption from the battery level

A second, independent view of consumption: the hourly battery level from Home Assistant is split into trip segments between two charges. A segment starts at the highest level after a charge and ends at the lowest level before the next one; the km come from the odometer at these times. Month by month, the figures are shown on the Statistics page.

What counts as a charge: a rise by the minimum charging rise (settings, default 5 percentage points) – measured from the lowest level, not from hour to hour. An AC charge creeping along overnight thus counts as one single charge, and the noise of the hourly average no longer splits a segment into one-per-cent snippets. Smaller top-ups do not split the segment, but they are not included in its kWh either – the consumption is then slightly too low.
Energy per segmentkWh = (battery start − battery end) [%] ÷ 100 × battery capacity [kWh]
Consumption per segmentConsumption = kWh ÷ (km end − km start) × 100
Example – battery from 80 % to 42 %, odometer from 12.310 to 12.455, 58,3 kWh battery: (80 − 42) ÷ 100 × 58,3 = 22,15 kWh 22,15 ÷ 145 × 100 = 15,3 kWh/100 km
Without charging losses: what counts is what came out of the battery – not what flowed at the wallbox or charging station. The value is therefore below the consumption from charging; the difference roughly equals the charging losses. Standby consumption between trips (pre-conditioning, 12 V battery) is included.
Accuracy: HA provides the battery level as an hourly average – per segment this is about ±1 % (±0,6 kWh) inaccurate. Segments under 5 km therefore do not count in the monthly values. If the battery consumption is above the one from charging, charging sessions are probably missing (charged away from home, no receipt recorded).

Update: every night for the last 45 days. The entire history can be recalculated with a button on the Trips page. Requires the sensors battery level and odometer in the settings; the battery capacity (default 58,3 kWh) can also be set there.

Charging tariffs ↑ back to top

Heading: “🏷️ Charging tariffs” · your subscriptions for public charging, e.g. EnBW M.

  • Per tariff: ct/kWh AC and DC, base fee per month, blocking fee (ct/min, after how many minutes, cap), prices in third-party networks (roaming) and the charging card.
  • Price change = new entry with a new “valid from”. The table shows the change compared with the previous entry, the chart the history next to your home electricity price.
  • Only fill in “valid until” if you have cancelled the subscription.
  • Base fee: counts in every month in which the tariff applies – even without charging. If the tariff starts or ends during the month, it is calculated pro rata by days (e.g. from the 16th of a 30-day month: 15/30 of the fee).
  • Charging sessions are assigned to the tariff via the provider name and the date.
Effective priceeffective [ct/kWh] = (Σ charging costs of the month + base fee) ÷ Σ kWh × 100
Base fee in the costs: it is included in the electricity costs, the Ø electricity price and the savings on the dashboard, statistics and in the reports – as an entry without kWh that does not count as a charging session. With few charges in a month, the effective price is clearly above the tariff price – then a smaller tariff may be worthwhile.

{{ kf.name }} ↑ back to top

Heading: “⛽ {{ kf.name }} prices (monthly average)”

  • One price in €/L per month – the reference for the entire savings calculation.
  • Normally comes automatically from HA (Tankerkönig monthly average), but can be overwritten.
Ø {{ kf.name }} price (dashboard)Ø = Σ of all monthly prices ÷ number of months
Unweighted: every recorded month counts the same, regardless of the distance driven. If no price has been recorded yet, the app uses 1,80 €/L instead.
Different in reports: there, only the months of the report period are averaged – a monthly report therefore uses exactly the price of that month.

Electricity tariff ↑ back to top

Heading: “⚡ Electricity tariff history”

  • With every tariff change, add a new entry with “valid from”; old ones are kept as history.
  • The entry with the latest date is the current tariff.
  • This price is used for grid charging – also with the automatic import.
What a tariff entry recalculates: imported monthly grid totals are revalued with the tariff of their month as soon as a tariff is added or deleted. Manually recorded charging sessions and months with a dynamic tariff (below) keep their price. If there is no tariff at all, 30 ct/kWh is used for the import.

Dynamic electricity tariff (Tibber, aWATTar, Octopus …)

With a tariff whose price changes (hourly or every quarter of an hour) no fixed value fits. For this there is the optional sensor “Grid cost into car (€)”: a running counter in Home Assistant that adds the price at that moment to every kWh charged from the grid. The template ev_netzkosten.yaml creates it as sensor.ev_ladung_netz_kosten.

Price of the monthPrice [ct/kWh] = costs of the month [€] ÷ grid into car [kWh] × 100
  • The monthly total gets the note “dynamischer Tarif” and the actual costs as total price.
  • If the cost value for a month is missing (sensor created mid-month, gap) or gives an implausible price (above 150 ct/kWh or not above 0), the electricity tariff of this page applies – the import log names the reason.
  • The electricity tariff still makes sense: for months before the cost meter, the price suggestion for manual charging and the simulation.

Individual charging sessions from Home Assistant

Instead of just a monthly total, Home Assistant can send each charging session at home individually – with date, time, kWh from grid and PV and (with a cost meter) the costs. Switch it on under Settings → Receive charging sessions from Home Assistant; the template ev_ladung_senden.yaml remembers the meter readings at the start of charging and sends the differences at the end.

Monthly totalRemainder [kWh] = meter value of the month − Σ single sessions of the month
  • The single sessions carry the note “HA-Ladung 22:10–05:30”. Grid is valued with the costs sent, otherwise with the electricity tariff of the day; PV with the PV price.
  • The nightly fetch only creates the remainder as a monthly total (note “Rest ohne Einzelladung”). If it is less than 0,5 kWh, the monthly total is dropped.
  • If a session did not arrive (add-on just restarted), it is included in the remainder – nothing is missing, only the date is then the first of the month.
  • The same session may arrive several times; its ID is the start of charging. The template splits a session across the turn of the month at midnight.

Tax & GHG ↑ back to top

Heading: “💶 Vehicle tax & GHG quota”

Tax savingsSavings [€] = vehicle tax {{ kf.fahrzeug }} × months ÷ 12 − EV vehicle tax × taxable months ÷ 12

Both amounts are annual values and are calculated pro rata by months in the period. As long as the EV is tax-exempt, its tax stays at 0 €.

End of the tax exemption: under “EV vehicle tax” enter the annual amount from the tax notice and the first taxable month. It is deducted only from that month – earlier periods stay unchanged. If the amount changes later, the new value applies to all months from the month entered.
GHG incomeGHG income [€] = Σ of all recorded amounts

Each payout as its own entry with date – so it ends up in the right monthly and annual report.

Maintenance ↑ back to top

Heading: “🔧 Maintenance” – in the menu under Costs

One entry per invoice: inspection, tyres, brakes, wear parts, repair, MOT/emissions test, care or other. The odometer reading is optional and only serves as an overview.

€ per 100 kmMaintenance [€/100 km] = Σ costs ÷ km driven × 100

The km come from the monthly values of the Trips page, per year or in total. There is deliberately no comparison with a {{ kf.fahrzeug }} – realistic reference data is missing for that.

km of the previous car: if the Trips page still contains km of the old car, the value is too low. When changing vehicles, clear the trips via “Reset measurements” (Backup). Maintenance and insurance are not included in the savings on the dashboard.

Insurance ↑ back to top

Heading: “🛡️ Car insurance” – in the menu under Costs

One entry per insurance year with vehicle, insurer, cover, no-claims classes, excess, annual mileage, base premium and add-ons. If something changes, add a new entry with a new “valid from” – the table then shows the change compared with the previous year of the same vehicle.

Annual premiumTotal [€/year] = base premium + driver protection + approved repairer + cover abroad + breakdown cover + other

Add-ons: empty = not booked, 0 = included in the tariff, negative = discount (typical for the approved-repairer option).

Mileagedriven [km/year] = Σ km of the last 12 completed months (fewer months: extrapolated)

If the value is above the agreed annual mileage, a red warning appears – then notify the insurer, otherwise you may have to pay extra in the event of a claim.

HA Import ↑ back to top

Heading: “📥 Data import – Home Assistant / database”

Nightly fetch

Runs automatically at the set time (default 00:00, can be changed under Reports) and fetches the current and the previous month – so the previous month is still completed if data arrives late. “Run now” triggers it manually.

ValueTargetBehaviour
KilometresMonthly kmOverwrites the monthly value
{{ kf.name }} priceMonthly priceOverwrites the monthly value
PV kWhCharging session “Privat – PV”Creates or updates
Grid kWhCharging session “Privat – Netzbezug”Creates or updates
Manual entries stay untouched: the automatic fetch only updates its own bookings on the first day of the month.

Log

Every fetch is recorded with a time stamp – automatic and manual. For each month there are two lines:

  • gelesen (read) – what the sensors returned. A — means: no value received.
  • übernommen (applied) – what was actually written to the database.

This lets you tell whether a sensor returns nothing or the value was just not applied. The log is shortened automatically when it gets too long. It is always written in German.

Retroactive fetch

Any months can be loaded via the period selection. The values first appear in a preview where they can be edited; an empty field is not applied. When applying, charging sessions that already exist are skipped as duplicates.

Data sources

The default is the Home Assistant API (long-term statistics). If you also collect your sensor data in a database, you can choose it as a source – each value is then looked up there first; if it returns nothing, the HA API steps in automatically. This is worthwhile above all if the database goes back further than HA.

Finding a sensor in the database: in the settings under “Sensors” enter a search term (e.g. “odometer” or “kilometer”) and click “Search in database”. The search uses the saved connection – so after entering the credentials, save first. The results show name or entity ID, unit and – except for Prometheus – from when to when data exists. This also shows whether a gap in the import already exists in the database. “Apply” enters the result in the selected row; then save.

Sensor renamed? If the name of a sensor has changed (e.g. after switching the vehicle integration), enter both names separated by |, the old one first: Ceed Kilometerstand | New name. The values of both names are merged – for the odometer, the month of the rename is then correct as well (reading under the new name minus reading under the old name). This works in both columns; for the HA API, with several entity IDs the first one that returns values applies.

Where does a value come from? The import preview marks each value with DB (database) or HA (Home Assistant). If the database returns nothing, the reason is shown below in grey – e.g. “no values in the month”, “no value before the month” (the difference needs the reading before), “gap” (the last reading before is more than 45 days ago – the difference would be the distance of several months; then the HA API steps in, otherwise spread the km of the gap over the months by hand) or the error message of the database. The same is shown in the import log.

New since 2.1.0: InfluxDB 2.x, InfluxDB 3.x (since 2.5.0), PostgreSQL/TimescaleDB and Prometheus/VictoriaMetrics are tested, but not yet with many real installations. If something does not fit, the “… test” button in the settings shows the error message of the database – please report it with this message as an issue on GitHub.
How it is calculated: km and kWh as the difference of the meter reading (last value in the month minus last value before, up to a year back); if that returns nothing, for kWh the sum of the daily maximums (for meters reset daily). {{ kf.name }} price as the mean of all values above 0. Battery level and odometer for the battery consumption as hourly values.

Invoices ↑ back to top

Heading: “📄 Invoice import” · for charging away from home.

Reports ↑ back to top

Heading: “📊 Reports by e-mail” · the reports are sent in the language set in the settings.

Month-end check

“Check month” reports whether the month can be evaluated. It checks: are kilometres recorded, is a {{ kf.name }} price entered, are there charging sessions – and are there charges detected from the battery history without a matching receipt?

Charge detection: a rise of the battery level by at least 5 percentage points counts as a charging session. The app estimates the energy as rise [%] ÷ 100 × battery capacity (default 58,3 kWh). First, receipts from the road are matched via the date (± 1 day). The remaining charges are offset against the kWh charged at home in the month – these come from HA only as a monthly total (+5 % margin for the estimate). A missing charge therefore only shows up when it is larger than the charging losses at home in the month (about 10 % of home charging). Whatever is left was probably a charge away from home – the receipt is still missing.

Contents of the reports

Monthly and annual reports contain key figures with a comparison to the previous period, the breakdown by provider and – for the annual report – the monthly history.

Costs per 100 kmCosts [€/100km] = electricity costs ÷ km × 100

Automatic sending

  • The monthly report goes out from the 1st of the following month, the annual report from 1 January – each once.
  • If “Only send when the month is complete” is active, the app waits up to the set number of days. After that the report goes out anyway with a note on the gaps.

Backup ↑ back to top

Heading: “💾 Backup & restore”

  • A cron job on the Docker host backs up the database daily to OneDrive via rclone.
  • “Trigger backup now” only creates a marker – the cron job starts within a few minutes.
  • The status counts as up to date as long as the last backup is less than 26 hours old.
  • “Download database” provides a consistent copy, even while the app is writing.
Restoring replaces the running database. The previous one is first backed up automatically as vor_restore_….db. The uploaded file is checked – only real EV Tracker databases are accepted.
“Read-only” after a downgrade: the database remembers its structure level. If it comes from a newer version of EV Tracker (e.g. after going back to an older add-on version), the app opens it read-only: all pages are visible, but nothing is saved, imported or sent by e-mail – an orange banner points this out. Otherwise the older version could write the newer structure incorrectly (e.g. kilometres twice). Remedy: install the new version again or restore a backup from the older version here – restoring stays allowed.

Reset measurements – for a change of vehicle or to remove test data:

  • Each area can be selected individually: kilometres driven, charging sessions, {{ kf.name }} prices, battery segments, GHG entries. Empty areas cannot be ticked.
  • The button does not delete anything yet – it opens a confirmation that lists each area with the exact number. Only “Yes, delete permanently” carries it out.
  • Settings, electricity tariffs, charging providers and the HA configuration are always kept.
  • The database is first backed up as vor_reset_….db in the data folder.
The nightly fetch rewrites kilometres, {{ kf.name }} prices and battery segments from Home Assistant. After the reset, first switch the sensors in the settings to the new vehicle – otherwise the old values will be back the next morning.

Several vehicles ↑ back to top

Settings → “🚗 Vehicles” – the default is “One vehicle”: then everything looks as always.

  • Switching on: choose “Several vehicles” and confirm. First the database is backed up as vor_mehrere_fahrzeuge_….db, then two tables are converted so that each vehicle has its own months. The values do not change.
  • Per vehicle: name, picture, comparison combustion car (fuel, consumption, CO2), vehicle tax (combustion car and EV), battery size, odometer and battery sensor, km driven, charging sessions, GHG, maintenance and insurance. Shared remain electricity tariff, fuel prices, PV price, charging providers, mail and data source.
  • Switcher at the top right: one vehicle or “All vehicles”. The total view calculates each vehicle with its own comparison values and adds them up; consumption, ct/kWh and values per 100 km are formed from the totals – never as an average. A table shows each vehicle.
  • Recording in the total view: forms ask which vehicle the entry belongs to. When editing a charging session, it can be assigned to another vehicle.
  • Vehicle tax counts for each vehicle only for its own months – a car bought later gets no savings for the time before.
Home chargingHow it is counted
Shared wallbox – split by kmThe meters under “Sensors” belong to all vehicles using it. The monthly total is split by the km driven in the month (without km in equal parts) – an estimate, the note names the share.
Shared wallbox – single sessions via pushHome Assistant sends each charging session with a vehicle ID (blueprint “EV Tracker – Ladung senden” or template, field fahrzeug: id or name). Only the remainder without single sessions is split by km.
Shared wallbox – assignment via battery levelEV Tracker places the hourly values of the wallbox next to the battery level of each car (battery sensor). If in an hour the battery rises for exactly one car (± 2 hours margin, because many manufacturer clouds report the battery level late), the energy belongs to this car. If both charge at the same time or the battery level is missing, the hour goes into the remainder – which is split by km. The note shows both, e.g. “86 % über Akkustand, Rest 62 % nach km” (86 % via battery level, remainder 62 % by km). No setup in Home Assistant needed.
Own metersThe vehicle has its own kWh meters (own wallbox). They are shown under “Sensors” when this vehicle is selected at the top.
ShareVehicle share [%] = km of the vehicle in the month ÷ km of all vehicles using the shared wallbox × 100
Which car is charging right now? The wallbox knows best: one RFID card per car (go-e, Easee, Wallbox and others) or evcc with vehicle detection. Enter its sensor in the blueprint under “Lädt dieses Auto?” (is this car charging; state = card ID or vehicle name). Charging status or plug of the vehicle integration also work, but via the manufacturer cloud they often arrive 15–60 minutes late – then assignment via the battery level is the better choice.

The base fee of a charging tariff belongs to one vehicle or to all (“shared”) – then it is also split by km.

Back to one vehicle – nothing is deleted. You choose which vehicle stays visible; the others are only hidden. Their data stays in the database, in backups and exports and is back immediately after switching back. Nothing is imported for hidden vehicles; push sessions for them are rejected (instead of ending up with the wrong car). Deleting is a separate action in the vehicle list: only after entering the name, a backup is made first (vor_fahrzeug_loeschen_….db).
Older add-on version: after switching to “Several”, a version before 3.0 can only read the database (banner “Read-only”). Going back is possible via the backup vor_mehrere_fahrzeuge_….db under Backup. If you stay with one vehicle, you are not affected.

Key figures in detail ↑ back to top

Fuel savings

Step 1 – notional {{ kf.name }} costs{{ kf.name }} costs = Σ per month ( km ÷ 100 × consumption × {{ kf.name }} price of the month )
Step 2 – savingsFuel savings = {{ kf.name }} costs − Σ of all charging costs
Example – two months, 7,0 L/100 km, no charging costs recorded yet: Month 1: 1.500 ÷ 100 × 7,0 = 105,00 L × 1,80 €/L = 189,00 € Month 2: 1.788 ÷ 100 × 7,0 = 125,16 L × 1,75 €/L = 219,03 € 189,00 + 219,03 − 0,00 = 408,03 €
Ø {{ kf.name }} price is therefore km-weighted: {{ kf.name }} costs ÷ litres (in the example 408,03 ÷ 230,16 = 1,773 €/L). A month with 3.000 km counts more than one with 100 km. Months without a recorded price get the average of all recorded monthly prices.
As long as charging costs are missing, the savings are too high. They then equal the full {{ kf.name }} costs. Only with recorded charging does the value become realistic.

Total savings

TotalTotal savings = fuel savings + vehicle tax + GHG income

Not included: insurance, servicing, tyres, depreciation. Only energy and tax costs are compared; the purchase is handled separately in the payback.

Payback of the extra cost

An EV usually costs more to buy than a comparable {{ kf.fahrzeug }}. The payback shows how much of this extra cost the ongoing total savings have already recovered – and when the rest is likely to follow. Enter the prices under Settings → Purchase – payback; without a purchase price for the EV it stays hidden.

Extra costExtra cost = purchase price EV − price of a comparable {{ kf.fahrzeug }} − subsidies
StatusRecovered [%] = total savings so far ÷ extra cost × 100 · paid off in the first month in which the cumulative total savings reach the extra cost
ForecastMonths until payback = (extra cost − total savings so far) ÷ average total savings per month, rounded up
Example – purchase price 44.990 €, comparable {{ kf.fahrzeug }} 36.000 €, subsidies 3.000 €: Extra cost: 44.990 − 36.000 − 3.000 = 5.990 € Total savings so far 2.641 € → 2.641 ÷ 5.990 = 44 %, 3.349 € to go Average 115 € per month → 3.349 ÷ 115 = 29,1 → 30 months
Always over the entire period: the period selection at the top of the dashboard does not change the payback. The forecast uses the average of the last 12 completed months – the current month is usually still incomplete. It does not project beyond 15 years. With several vehicles the overall view shows the total of all vehicles with a purchase price.
Not included: depreciation, servicing, insurance and financing costs. An EV with a higher resale value therefore pays off earlier, one with higher depreciation later than the curve shows.

CO2 savings

Avoided CO2CO2 [kg] = km ÷ 100 × {{ kf.fahrzeug }} consumption × CO2 factor [kg/L]
Gross value: what is calculated is what the {{ kf.fahrzeug }} would have emitted. The emissions of the electricity charged are not deducted – the real advantage is therefore lower, unless only PV electricity was charged.

Consumption kWh/100 km

Charging losses included: the calculation uses the energy charged at the charging point, not at the battery – the value is typically 5–15 % above the on-board computer display. As imported charging is booked on the first of the month, individual months can also look distorted; over several months this evens out.

Sensor values from HA ↑ back to top

How a sensor becomes a monthly value – this explains most deviations in the import.

ValueMethodMeaning
OdometerDifferenceLast reading of the month minus last reading of the previous month.
PV into carSumFor meters reset daily; otherwise also difference.
Grid into carDifferenceAs with the odometer.

Two routes per value: first the long-term statistics are read (accurate, but only available if the sensor keeps them). If they return nothing, the history API steps in and looks for the last value before the start of the month – up to twelve months back. This is necessary for sensors that rarely update.
If a value stays empty: usually the entity ID is wrong, the sensor keeps no long-term statistics, or the history does not go back far enough. The log on the HA Import page shows which values were read; the diagnosis in the settings checks a sensor over three months.

Simulation mode ↑ back to top

For everyone who still drives a {{ kf.fahrzeug }}: the app shows live what an EV would save with the same kilometres. Switch it on under Settings → Simulation mode.

ValueSource
Kilometresreal – odometer of the current car, as usual from Home Assistant or the database
{{ kf.name }} pricereal – e.g. Tankerkönig; so the {{ kf.name }} costs are no longer an extrapolation but the actual ones
Electricity demandsimulated – km ÷ 100 × EV reference (kWh/100 km) × (1 + charging losses)
Electricity costssimulated – split by the shares PV (PV price), grid (electricity tariff of the month) and public (ct/kWh price set)
Simulated charging per monthkWh = km ÷ 100 × EV consumption × (1 + charging losses) · costs = Σ kWh × share × price

Dashboard, statistics, charts and e-mail report calculate with it as with real charging; there is a note at the top of every page, and the e-mail report carries “(simulation)” in the title. Nothing is stored – the list under Charging stays empty or only shows real charging sessions.

After the purchase: switch off the simulation. From then on the real charging sessions count. The statistics additionally show “Forecast vs. actual”: what the simulation would have predicted for the same km, next to the real values – so you can see how good the assumptions were.
Assumptions: the EV consumption is constant over the year – in winter it is really 20–30 % higher. If you let the simulation run through summer and winter, you still get a good annual picture if the value set matches the annual average.

FAQ ↑ back to top

The fuel savings seem too high.

Charging costs are probably missing. As long as no charging is recorded, the savings equal the full {{ kf.name }} costs. Check the HA import and add charging away from home.

A month is missing in the monthly chart.

The {{ kf.name }} price or the kilometres are missing for this month. The chart only shows months that occur in both data sets.

The consumption is higher than on the on-board computer.

Correct – the calculation uses the energy charged including charging losses.

The nightly fetch returns no values.

First look at the log on the HA Import page: if “gelesen” (read) shows — everywhere, nothing arrives. Then test the connection in the settings and open the sensor diagnosis. Most common causes: wrong entity ID or missing long-term statistics.

I changed the {{ kf.fahrzeug }} consumption – why do old values change?

{{ kf.name }} equivalent and CO2 are never stored but recalculated with every display. The parameter therefore affects the entire history.

We have two EVs – does that work?

Yes, switch to “Several vehicles” under Settings → Vehicles, see Several vehicles. If both share a wallbox, home charging is split by km – it gets more accurate with single sessions via push, assignment via battery level or own meters.

Can I switch the language back?

Yes, at any time with DE | EN at the top right or under Settings → Calculation parameters. Nothing in your data changes.

Where is my data stored?

In an SQLite file in the container's data directory, backed up to OneDrive via cron job. It can be downloaded at any time via the Backup page.
